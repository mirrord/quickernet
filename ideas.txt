imagine a hierarchy of concepts, each abstraction pulling from the levels below it (and to a lesser extent, other places as well)
data starts "raw" from the sensors, and systematically processed by several rounds of clustering, anomaly detection, convolution,
self-association, and retro-association to produce each progressive level of abstractions - first into basic features, then
combinations of those features, and so on. At the lower end, we have lines, high contrast, or other simple ideas little more
than patterns in the sensory data, while at the higher end we have "family," and "self," and "organization." 
I hypothesize that our emotions are our brain's current most abstract heuristic estimation of the models above these concepts and
the brain's experimentation of how each of the highest concepts (in turn, moving down the hierarchy) can assist in predicting
proceeding input. Each emotion is really an estimation of how related each collection of related "concept webs" will be most
promissing for including in current predictions.
